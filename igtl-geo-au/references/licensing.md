# What the IGA allows, licences, jurisdictions and operators without an Australian licence

Read this file in any task about a specific product or operator: when you need to establish whether a product is permitted; when the text names an operator, brand or domain, mentions a licence, legality or a regulator; when it is about an offshore operator, online casino, pokies, poker or ACMA blocking.

Sources and commencement dates of rules are in `sources.md`.

## 1. IGA product category

The Interactive Gambling Act 2001 (IGA) prohibits providing "prohibited interactive gambling services" to people in Australia and requires nothing of the player: the prohibitions are addressed to those who provide and advertise the service. Establish the category before writing about the product [Requirement].

| Product online (website, app) | Status for people in Australia | Basis |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Betting on sport before the event starts with an operator holding an Australian licence | Permitted | excluded wagering service, IGA s 8A |
| Betting on racing (thoroughbred, harness, greyhound) with an operator holding an Australian licence | Permitted | IGA s 8A |
| Betting on sport after the event starts (in-play) via a website or app | Prohibited | IGA s 8A(3)(b), s 10B |
| In-play on sport by phone, by voice only | Permitted | telephone betting service, IGA s 8AA |
| In-play on sport in person at a venue where the provider is licensed under state or territory law, through equipment available to any customer at that venue | Permitted | place-based betting service, IGA s 8BA; ACMA applied the exception to terminals and the Tap in-play service in Tabcorp venues |
| Online casino: pokies (slots), roulette, blackjack, baccarat, live casino, game shows | Prohibited; no Australian licence exists | IGA s 5, s 15 |
| Online poker, including peer-to-peer | Prohibited; no Australian licence exists | IGA s 4 (game of chance or mixed chance and skill), s 5 |
| Betting on the outcome of a lottery (lottery betting) | Prohibited | ACMA, list of prohibited services |
| Online keno (keno-type lottery) and foreign matched lotteries | Prohibited from 01.01.2027 | Gambling Reform Act 2026, Schedule 4 |
| Betting with a bookmaker without an Australian licence (offshore sportsbook) | Prohibited (unlicensed regulated interactive gambling service) | IGA s 15AA; ACMA |
| Credit to customers of online bookmakers, payment by credit card, credit wallet or cryptocurrency | Prohibited | IGA Part 2B, s 15C |
| State lotteries | Permitted; outside the skill's scope | — |

- **In-play on racing.** The IGA in-play ban is framed for sporting events; according to lawyers' commentary, an online bet after a race starts is not covered by it (secondary source). Races last minutes; do not describe "live racing betting" in an app unless the operator itself offers it [Fact].
- **Quick codes for calls (Fast Codes, Quick Codes).** In 2023 ACMA found schemes in breach where the details of an in-play bet are selected in the app and the call only confirms it: the bet details must be given by voice. Do not describe "one-click" in-play through an app as a phone bet.
- **An operator with an Australian licence cannot offer an online casino.** If casino games appear in a text about a licensed Australian brand, this is either an error in the text or a discrepancy to report to the person who set the task.
- **Demo versions** of games without real-money stakes are not gambling under the IGA definition. But a demo page that leads to an offshore casino remains advertising for that casino (section 6).
- Do not write "online gambling is legal in Australia" or "online casinos are legal for Australians" as a general statement. Be precise: what is permitted, with whom and by what means.

## 2. Jurisdictions and regulators

- **Federal level:** the IGA. ACMA handles complaints, investigates breaches, asks providers to block illegal websites, keeps the register of licensed operators and the BetStop self-exclusion register. ACMA does not issue licences.
- **States and territories** license betting and set rules for their licensees and advertising rules in their territory. Most online bookmakers are licensed in the Northern Territory under the Racing and Wagering Act 2024 (NT, in force from 01.07.2024). Other authorities also appear in the ACMA register: the Victorian Gambling and Casino Control Commission, Liquor & Gaming NSW, the NT Director-General of Licensing and others; check the register to see which authority licensed a particular brand.
- **Name of the NT authority** [Requirement: Racing and Wagering Amendment Act 2026 (NT), No. 12 of 2026, in force from 22.06.2026]: the former Northern Territory Racing and Wagering Commission was renamed the **Northern Territory Wagering Commission**; its racing functions were removed. Before 01.07.2024 the authority was the Northern Territory Racing Commission. As at 13.11.2026 the ACMA register still showed the former name; on 24.11.2026 the register entries seen (bet365, Sportsbet) show the current name. If an entry shows a former name, this is a conflict between official sources: for the current name rely on the authority itself and the current version of the law, and note the discrepancy in the report. If the entry shows the current name, there is no conflict to report. The Department of Tourism and Hospitality (NT) publishes its own list of NT licensees ("Licensed wagering operators": company, trading name, website).
- **Advertising rules depend on where the text is accessible.** A text on a website is accessible in every state, so the rules of each may apply to it; the strictest and best known in practice is the NSW ban on advertising inducements (`products.md`, "Inducements").
- Do not write "licensed by the Australian government", "ACMA-licensed" or "national gambling licence". There is no single national regulator and no single licence.
- New Zealand is a separate jurisdiction. Do not apply Australian rules to it or vice versa.

## 3. Checking status in the ACMA register

The licence is confirmed by a current entry in the ACMA register of licensed operators (the page "Check if a gambling operator is legal") or in the licensing authority's official register (for the NT, the Northern Territory Wagering Commission list of licensed wagering operators), with matching brand and legal entity. The report names the register actually used. An ACMA register entry has four columns: Trading name, Licence holder, URL, Licensing authority. An older ACMA page with the same register shows three columns without URL; rely on the current page.

The check consists of separate parts, and their results are recorded separately. Part 3 is needed only if the text names, advertises or links to a specific mobile app.

**Part 1. Licence of the brand and legal entity**
1. **Brand**: the trading name matches the brand.
2. **Legal entity**: the licence holder matches the company in the operator's Australian T&Cs.
3. **Licensing authority**: shown in the ACMA entry or evident from the authority's own register; if needed, compare the two. The name of the NT authority follows section 2.
4. **Product**: a betting licence covers betting only; no one's licence covers an online casino (section 1).

"No current URL" in the URL column does not affect this part: the register includes licensees without a listed website, for example phone bookmakers.

**Part 2. Link between the domain being checked and the licensee**
1. **URL column**: the domain matches the URL in the entry → link confirmed.
2. **No match or "No current URL"**: also check the licensing authority's official list (the NT list has a website column). A match there → link confirmed.
3. **Confirmed in neither place**: the link between the domain and the licensee is not confirmed. T&Cs and the footer on the domain itself do not confirm it: ACMA blocks sites imitating licensed brands (for example, LightningBet, PointsBet, ChromaBet).

**Part 3. Link between an app and the licensee** [iGTL Rule]

A confirmed website does not in itself confirm an app with the brand's name: app stores contain copies and apps from other publishers. The app is checked separately, and all three conditions must match.
1. **Link from the confirmed website**: the domain whose link to the licensee is confirmed in Part 2, or an official subdomain of it (for example, the operator's help centre), links to the app's page in the App Store or Google Play; the link need not be on the `www` site. If Part 2 is not confirmed, Part 3 is not confirmed either.
2. **Identifier**: the identifier of the app being checked matches the identifier in that link: in the App Store, the number after `id` in the page address; in Google Play, the package name after `id=`. Name, icon, screenshots and description do not count as an identifier.
3. **Publisher**: the developer (developer, seller) on the app page matches the licence holder in the ACMA register or the legal entity in the Australian T&Cs on the confirmed website. If the publisher is different (for example, a group company), the link is not confirmed; the question goes in `[VERIFY-n]`.

Check the app page in the Australian storefront (in the App Store address, `/au/`; in Google Play, the parameter `gl=AU`). An app found by searching the store by name, without an identifier match with the link on the confirmed website, is not confirmed. Installation files outside the stores (an APK by direct link) cannot be confirmed this way; if the text mentions them, the question goes in `[VERIFY-n]`.

**Source**: the register entry, the authority's list, website and app store pages, check dates. If the ACMA register page does not open, use the licensing authority's official register (or ACMA site search); a matching entry there confirms Part 1, and the report names that register. If neither can be checked, the Part 1 result is "insufficient data".

Licence status is a dynamic critical fact (`SKILL.md`, section 3). If time has passed between the check and publication, open the entry again; give the date of the last check in the report.

Status is not inferred from signs: a .com.au domain, prices in Australian dollars, a BetStop logo, 1800 858 858 in the footer, betting on AFL and NRL prove neither that a licence exists nor that it does not.

## 4. Check results

| Check | Possible results |
| :---- | :---- |
| Licence of the brand and legal entity (Part 1) | current licence confirmed; insufficient data; officially confirmed (cancellation, suspension, illegal service) |
| Link between the domain and the licensee (Part 2) | confirmed (URL in the register or in the authority's list); not confirmed |
| Link between the app and the licensee (Part 3, if the text names an app) | confirmed (link from the confirmed website, identifier and publisher match); not confirmed |

Each result is stated separately in the report. If the licence is confirmed but the domain link is not: describe the licence as the licence of the brand and legal entity (section 5), but do not state that the site being checked is that licensee's service; the question goes in `[VERIFY-n]` or a manual check item. If the app link is not confirmed: do not call it the brand's official app and do not link to its store page; "not confirmed" means only that the link could not be confirmed, and does not mean the app is fake or unconnected to the operator; if the website link is confirmed, you may send the reader to the operator's website for the app ("download the app via [Brand]'s website"). If the text is about a brand without a specific website (for example, a phone bookmaker), Part 2 is not needed; if the text does not name an app, Part 3 is not needed.

**Licence of the brand and legal entity:**
- **Current licence confirmed**: the brand and legal entity are found in the ACMA register or in the licensing authority's official register, and the licensing authority is known → wording from the table below; the report names the register used.
- **Insufficient data**: the brand or legal entity does not match or is not found, or the register could not be checked → the Australian licence is not confirmed. Continue working to the brief without attributing an Australian licence or the legality of the offer to the operator. List the missing confirmations in the report. The absence of an entry under the expected name does not in itself prove there is no licence: the brand may be registered under another trading name. Per ACMA guidance, services not on the register should not be used, and a complaint about such a service goes to ACMA.
- **Officially confirmed**: there is a licensing authority decision cancelling or suspending the licence, an ACMA notice about blocking the site, a formal warning or an ACMA investigation report about providing an illegal service → section 6. **State the specific status and source**: "ACMA requested ISPs to block [domain] ([date of the notice])", "licence cancelled by [authority] on [date]". Do not reduce it to a general "no licence".

The client's word that a licence exists or does not exist, without such confirmation, gives the result "insufficient data".

For online casinos, pokies and poker, no register check is needed for the licence conclusion: no Australian licence exists for these products by law. Only check whether ACMA has published a blocking notice for the domain: this is a dynamic fact for the report.

**Single confirmation rule:**
- holding an Australian betting licence is confirmed only by a current entry in the ACMA register or in the licensing authority's official register, with matching brand and legal entity; the link between a specific domain and the licensee by the register's URL column or the authority's official list; the link to an app by a link from the confirmed domain with matching identifier and publisher (Part 3);
- cancellation, suspension or illegality of a service is confirmed by an official source;
- an unavailable register does not cancel an official notice that was found.

If neither register can be reached by any route and there is no official notice, the result is "insufficient data". Do not write that you checked a register you did not open and do not give a check date for it.

## 5. Wording

| What is confirmed | Wording | Not allowed |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Brand and legal entity in the ACMA register | "[Brand] is operated by [Licence holder], which is licensed by the [Licensing authority]." | "licensed by ACMA", "ACMA-licensed" |
| The same, mentioning the ACMA register (only if the entry was found there) | "[Brand] appears on the ACMA register of licensed interactive wagering service providers." | "ACMA-approved", "government-endorsed" |
| NT licence | "licensed in the Northern Territory by the Northern Territory Wagering Commission" | former names (Northern Territory Racing and Wagering Commission, Northern Territory Racing Commission) as current; if needed, "formerly the Northern Territory Racing and Wagering Commission" |
| Neutral, for reviews and FAQ | "holds an Australian wagering licence issued by the [Licensing authority]" | "legal in Australia" without the product; extending the licence to casino games |
| Licence not confirmed | do not write about an Australian licence | see sections 4 and 6 |

- Copy the official name of the register and its fields from the ACMA website; do not reconstruct them.
- Licences from Curaçao, Anjouan, the Malta Gaming Authority, Kahnawake, Gibraltar and other regulators are not an Australian licence. Do not present them as permission to serve people in Australia.
- Before writing, record what is being considered: the brand as a whole or a specific offer, product, domain or app.

## 6. Operators without an Australian licence and prohibited services

Applies to offshore bookmakers without a licence and to any online casinos, pokies, poker and in-play services addressed to people in Australia.

- Complete the task in the brief's format. A prohibited or unconfirmed status is not in itself a reason to refuse the text automatically. The decision to publish stays with the client and their lawyer.
- **Risk of advertising a prohibited service** [Requirement: IGA Part 7A]: advertising prohibited interactive gambling services and unlicensed regulated interactive gambling services in Australia is prohibited; ACMA blocks operators' websites and affiliate websites that publish such advertising. From 01.01.2027 [Gambling Reform Act 2026, Schedule 2; the ban on publication on social media is a primary source, the rest has ⚠ status and is used only as a risk item]: advertising on an online service is treated as published in Australia if the service is accessible to a user in Australia; publication of such advertising on social media is separately prohibited; ACMA may require platforms, hosting providers and search engines to remove advertising, links and apps. If the text is addressed to people in Australia and promotes such a service, add the risk as one line in the report's manual checks. The status of such material is always Needs a human check; the client's decision to publish does not change the status (`SKILL.md`, section 8).
- **ACMA blocking** [Fact]: check whether the operator's domain appears in ACMA blocking notices. Do not put links to blocked domains or their mirrors in the text.
- **Bypassing** [iGTL Rule]: do not write instructions for bypassing ACMA blocking (VPN, mirrors, "new address after blocking"), do not position the operator as a way around BetStop or self-exclusion ("casinos not on BetStop", "no BetStop" as an advantage), do not address calls to action to self-excluded people or minors.
- **Legality and safety** [Requirement: ACL; iGTL Rule]: do not call the offer "legal in Australia", "Australian-licensed", "ACMA-approved", "safe and legal for Aussies". If the brief asks you to describe the player's position, be precise: the IGA prohibits offering and advertising such services to people in Australia; per ACMA's warning, users of illegal services are not protected by Australian law, including if the service does not pay out winnings; the operator is not connected to BetStop. The wording "it's not illegal for you to play" without these caveats misleads the reader; do not use it.
- **A foreign licence** is described from verified data: regulator, legal entity, entry in its register, link to the domain [Fact]. Do not write that it protects Australian players unless the regulator confirms this.
- **Features and terms** are described from verified data. Do not attribute Australian protection mechanisms (BetStop, NCPF limits, activity statements) to an offshore operator and do not present their absence as an advantage ("no deposit limits", "no ID needed").
- **Payments** [Requirement: Gambling Reform Act 2026, Schedule 2; ⚠ status, limits in `sources.md`]: from 01.01.2027 banks and payment systems must, as far as reasonably practicable, not process payments to illegal operators; until rechecked against the primary source, use this only as a risk item in the report. Do not promise funding through an Australian bank, PayID or card as reliable. AUSTRAC warns about "PayID pokies" schemes in which money goes to third parties' accounts: do not present such methods as ordinary and safe.
- **An unlicensed offshore sportsbook** is covered by the same points: betting with it is prohibited for people in Australia, although sports betting as a product is legal with licensees.
- Briefly state the check result and the legal category in the report. Do not repeat the legal explanation in every fragment.

The skill checks the text within its instructions and does not confirm that publishing it is legally permissible.
