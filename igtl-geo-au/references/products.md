# Betting, inducements, promotions and prohibited products

Read this file when the text describes betting on sport and racing, in-play, multi and same game multi, odds boosts, cash out, promotions, bonus bets, turnover, the promotion journey; and when the text concerns casino games, poker, lotteries, keno or trade promotions.

Check a specific operator's actual features separately; do not attribute restrictions or protection mechanisms to it just because they are listed here. First establish the IGA product category using `licensing.md`, section 1. Sources and statuses are in `sources.md`.

## Sports betting

- **Before the event starts**: a legal online product with an operator in the ACMA register [Requirement: IGA s 8A]. Markets, bet types, limits only from the operator's betting offer [Fact].
- **In-play** [Requirement: IGA s 8A(3)(b), s 10B, s 8AA, s 8BA; per ACMA guidance]: after a sporting event starts, a bet cannot be accepted online, through a website or app outside a venue. There are two exceptions:
  * **by phone**, if the bet details (selection, type, amount, confirmation) are given by voice; ACMA found schemes in breach where the bet is selected in the app and the call only confirms it;
  * **in person at a venue** (place-based betting service): the provider is licensed under state or territory law for that venue, and the equipment is available to any customer there. ACMA applied the exception to terminals in Tabcorp venues and, in 2026, to the Tap in-play service, calling the question finely balanced.
  A general explanation names both routes: "In-play bets on sport can't be placed online. They can be placed over the phone or in person at a licensed venue." A statement about a specific operator covers only what it offers itself, under its terms [Fact]: "[Brand] accepts in-play bets on sport over the phone". Do not write "live betting in the app" or "bet in-play online".
- **Live odds and in-play in NSW advertising** [Requirement: NSW, per the Liquor & Gaming NSW page]: publishing betting advertising connected with a sporting event under way in NSW, including live odds and in-play, is prohibited.
- **Cash out** [Fact: the feature and its terms; Requirement: NSW s 33H, per Liquor & Gaming NSW guidance in GL4015; iGTL Rule]. The feature appearing in T&Cs confirms only that it exists; T&Cs do not confirm that advertising wording is permitted. GL4015 lists advertising cash out that is available during the event among impermissible offers; descriptive advertising wording without a call to action can also be a problem ("cash out anytime during the game", "lock in your profit", "take control of your bet").
  * In advertising and affiliate text accessible in NSW, cash out is not promoted: no benefit, control or use during the event. A neutral mention of the feature in a list of features ("Cash out is available on selected markets. Terms apply.") is not automatically permitted: GL4015 prohibits certain cash out advertising but gives no separate permission for neutral wording. In advertising material such a mention stays only after the operator's compliance team has checked the context; until then, `[VERIFY-n]` and a manual check in the report.
  * In operator help content (FAQ, feature section, T&Cs) that does not serve as advertising, a factual description under the operator's terms, with no call to use the feature.
- **Multi and same game multi (SGM)** are the Australian names for accumulator and bet builder. Settlement rules follow the operator's betting rules [Fact].
- **Account restrictions:** do not promise that the operator does not restrict winning players unless it says so itself [Fact].
- Operator taxes and levies do not explain odds and margin without confirmation (`player-protection.md`, "Tax").

## Racing

- Thoroughbred (the gallops), harness (the trots), greyhound racing (the dogs) are legal online products with licensees [Requirement: IGA s 8A].
- **Fixed odds and tote** are different products. Describe "Best Tote", "Top Fluc", "SP" and similar strictly under the operator's rules [Fact].
- **Betting after the race starts**: `licensing.md`, section 1; do not state that it is available without confirmation from the operator.
- Race times with the time zone (`style-and-advertising.md`, section 3).

## Inducements

### What an inducement is

- **NSW** [per Liquor & Gaming NSW guidance]: any offer or benefit that may encourage a person to gamble, gamble more often, open an account or join a betting service.
- **From 01.01.2027, IGA s 62ZM** [Requirement: Gambling Reform Act 2026, Schedule 4A]: any payment or other benefit that could reasonably encourage a person to gamble; credit, gifts, rebates, rewards, tokens and vouchers count as a benefit.

### Basis [Requirement]

- **NSW, Betting and Racing Act 1998, s 33H:** the ban applies to any person, including affiliates and publishers: no one may publish or communicate, from NSW or from outside it, gambling advertising accessible in NSW with an inducement to participate, participate more often or open a betting account. A disclaimer that the offer is not available to NSW residents does not cure the breach. Inducements for referring a friend, keeping an account or agreeing to receive advertising are also prohibited [per Liquor & Gaming NSW guidance].
  * **Publisher defence** [per Liquor & Gaming NSW guidance in GL4015, section "Defence against prosecution for publishers"]: a publisher may defend against prosecution if the advertising was published in the form provided or approved by the operator (or its representative) and the publisher had not received a notice from the Minister or their delegate that the material may breach the law. After such a notice the defence does not apply. The skill can verify neither the operator's approval nor the absence of notices, so the iGTL rule for categories A and B (table below) is stricter than the law and does not depend on this defence.
- **Western Australia, Gaming and Wagering Commission Regulations 1988, reg 43** [per the WA Government guide "Gambling advertising and inducements", updated 18.06.2026]:
  * it is unlawful to advertise, offer or provide an inducement for opening an account or referring another person;
  * advertising may not offer an inducement to gamble or to keep gambling; the guide gives no exceptions for existing customers in published advertising. Offering and providing such inducements to existing customers is allowed;
  * publishing prohibited advertising is an offence for the publisher too, unless the publisher has the relevant operator's written approval of the publication; even with approval, the publisher is responsible for complying with codes of practice;
  * advertising with a disclaimer that the inducement is not available to WA residents is prohibited; advertising that reaches WA by accident is not treated as published in WA if it is aimed at an audience outside WA and the operator took reasonable steps to keep it from being seen in WA (for example, geoblocking);
  * direct messages to existing customers are not advertising if the operator itself (not a third-party affiliate) sends or displays them, the customer gave express consent and the messages contain a clear way to opt out, and for electronic messages a working unsubscribe link; after opting out, the messages stop;
  * trade promotion lotteries and dividends (without turnover conditions) are not inducements.
- **Victoria** [per VGCCC guidance; the section number of the Gambling Regulation Act 2003, s 4.7.10, from a secondary source]: an operator cannot offer credit, a voucher, a reward or another benefit as an inducement to open a betting account.
- **South Australia** [per SA Government guidance for the inducement ban; per the CBS code of practice for authorised betting operations, version from 01.01.2026, cl 11(3), 33(2)(c), 39 for the rest]: operators do not offer inducements and incentives to encourage a person to gamble or open an account, except for permitted exceptions; winnings from a complimentary gambling product (including a bonus bet) can be withdrawn without a requirement to keep betting with that money; a bonus bet may be advertised as a complimentary gambling product only when three conditions are met together: the offer applies only to bets on a racing event, the platform is primarily about racing, and the placement is in its racing-only section; direct marketing only with express consent, easy unsubscribe and stopping no later than 5 business days after it; consent to marketing or extra steps to opt out cannot be made a condition of using the account, and consent cannot be rewarded.
- **NCPF** [through state rules]: ban on inducements for opening an account and referring a friend; keeping an account cannot be rewarded.
- **IGA Part 7D** [Requirement: Gambling Reform Act 2026, Schedule 4A and Schedule 5, items 12A–12E]. Part 7D commences on 01.01.2027, but its provisions start to apply on different days:
  * **the commission ban (s 62ZT)** applies from 01.01.2027 to commissions offered or paid from that date (item 12C). A licensee cannot pay employees, agents and contractors who attract, retain or refer customers a commission linked to customers' activity. According to lawyers' commentary, this affects affiliates' revenue share and CPA [secondary source]; the skill does not assess payment schemes;
  * **the ban on offering inducements to designated customers** through direct marketing (s 62ZQ) and social media advertising (s 62ZR), the new-consent rule for direct marketing (s 62ZS), the definition of designated customer (s 62ZN(2)–(4)) and harm monitoring (s 62ZSA) apply from a day the Minister sets by notifiable instrument; that day must be later than 01.01.2027 (items 12A, 12B, 12D, 12E). Designated customers are customers whose account has been open for 14 days or less, who left BetStop in the last 90 days, customers at risk and for 90 days after leaving that status;
  * **the application date** and the result of the latest check: `sources.md`, section "Gambling Reform Act 2026". In the text and report, do not write that the ban on direct marketing of inducements applies from 01.01.2027; before relying on the date, check its current status (`SKILL.md`, section 3, "Dynamic norms"). If the check does not confirm that the day has been set, write in the text "applies from a day the Minister sets by notifiable instrument", and in the report "setting of the date not confirmed at the check on [date]", stating exactly what was checked.
  * The category A, B, C rule below applies regardless of these dates.
- Other states and territories have their own rules; the skill has not checked them point by point.

### Single rule for the text

The rule is the same for every part of the skill and for any text accessible in Australia, including texts about offshore operators. A note in the report never replaces deletion and never means clearance to publish.

The "What the law requires" column summarises the basis above; the "Skill rule" column is a mandatory iGTL rule: it ranks above the brief (`SKILL.md`, section 2) and is stricter than the law, because a text online is accessible in all states at once, and the skill cannot verify exceptions to the bans (geoblocking, the operator's written approval in WA, a logged-in area).

**Categories A, B and C are iGTL's editorial classification.** Putting an offer in a category does not in itself mean it breaches the law: the legal assessment depends on the mechanics of the specific offer and the publication context (GL4015, for example, distinguishes prohibited offers from some permitted special-odds variants). So in a remark give [iGTL Rule] as the basis; add [Requirement] only if the mechanics and the platform match a ban in the "Basis" section (for example, a sign-up offer in a text accessible in NSW).

- Examples from the Liquor & Gaming NSW guide GL4015 [per Liquor & Gaming NSW guidance]: impermissible offers include a refund of the stake if the horse runs second or third, bonus points that can be exchanged for bets, time-limited odds boosts, and an example with the words "Bet now" and a deposit bonus (the reason is the deposit bonus; the guide does not prohibit the words "Bet now" themselves); permissible: basic brand advertising and information about odds. Advertising a cash out feature available during the event is among impermissible offers (in the 2018 copy, the example "Have a break and cash out now"). Claims such as "best odds" are permitted only with confirmation (`style-and-advertising.md`, section 2).
- The "Bonuses" or "Promotions" section of a review for AU is filled with category C: for example, that public bonus advertising is prohibited in several states and existing customers see the terms in their account. Specific offers and amounts are not named.

### State conditions for promotions to existing customers

A list of questions for checking whether a specific category B offer is permissible. It is not a complete list of rules: for each state where the text is accessible, the operator's compliance team confirms permissibility.

- **South Australia** [per SA Government guidance for the inducement ban; per the CBS code of practice for authorised betting operations, version from 01.01.2026, cl 11(3), 33(2)(c), 39 for the rest]: operators do not offer inducements and incentives to encourage a person to gamble or open an account, except for permitted exceptions; winnings from a complimentary gambling product (including a bonus bet) can be withdrawn without a requirement to keep betting; a bonus bet may be advertised as a complimentary gambling product only for racing bets, on a platform primarily dedicated to racing and in its racing-only section; direct marketing only with express consent, easy unsubscribe and stopping no later than 5 business days after it, with no reward for consent.
- **Western Australia** [per the WA guide, updated 18.06.2026]: direct messages to existing customers who agreed to receive them are allowed and must contain help service contacts; on racing platforms you may not advertise, in particular, offers where an existing customer receives bonus bets above the original stake or deposit; a payout for a predetermined outcome (for example, second place) is not an inducement if the amount is immediately available for withdrawal without turnover conditions.
- **NSW** [Requirement: s 33H]: whether display in a logged-in area counts as publishing advertising with an inducement is for the operator's compliance team to decide; GL4015 separately covers digital material behind a login or paywall (the official PDF is not accessible to the tool).
- **Victoria and other states and territories:** the skill has not checked their conditions for existing customers; this goes to manual checks.
- **IGA Part 7D:** from a day the Minister sets, offering inducements to designated customers through direct marketing and on social media is prohibited (the "Basis" section above; check the current status of the date in the task).

## Describing promotions in the permitted case (category B in a logged-in area)

- **"Free", "risk-free", "money back"** [Requirement: ACL ss 18, 29]: in 2015 the Federal Court found advertising for "$200 FREE BETS" with deposit, turnover and exclusion conditions misleading. "Free" only if the terms do not contradict the word; "risk-free" only if the player cannot lose; "money back" only if the refund is in cash.
- **Bonus bet** [Fact]: Australian operators usually use this name for what GB calls a free bet. The stake of a bonus bet is generally not returned with the winnings; check and state it.
- **Turnover** [Fact]: Australia has no legal cap like the British 10x. Multiplier, base, minimum odds and deadline exactly as in the terms.
- **Material conditions** next to the offer; the small print does not contradict the headline [iGTL Rule; based on the ACL].
- **Deadline** only if confirmed, with the time zone.
- **Promotion minimum deposit** is a condition of that promotion; minimums by method come from the cashier.

### Offer currency [iGTL Rule]

Describe as available now only an offer that is current in Australia. Types: **current** (in the Australian terms or on the promotions page on the check date, not expired); **expired** (the period has passed, the direct URL may still open); **archived** (old version, cache, a retelling); **test or personal** (for some users, by code); **regional** (another country, .com version, state exclusions). Anything not current is not described as available; do not write "ongoing" without a basis.

### Full promotion journey [iGTL Rule]

| Stage | What to check |
| :---- | :---- |
| 1. Receiving and activating | Opt-in, promo code, qualifying bet, minimum odds, excluded methods and states; deadline and start event |
| 2. Using | Markets, minimum odds, maximum amount, bet type; deadline and start event; what happens to unused amounts |
| 3. Conditions for withdrawal | Turnover and its base, eligible markets, deadline, win cap, order in which funds are used, non-return of the bonus bet stake |

Stage deadlines are not merged into one. Something not found in the terms does not prove the condition does not exist: if the journey cannot be described without it, `[VERIFY-n]`. A direct link to the full terms goes in the published text next to the promotion; if unknown, `[VERIFY-n]`.

## Online casinos, pokies and poker (prohibited products)

Apply together with `licensing.md`, section 6.

- **Terminology:** "pokies" is the natural Australian word; "online pokies" denotes a product that may not be provided to Australians.
- **Licence:** only a foreign one, described exactly (`licensing.md`, section 5).
- **Casino promotions** follow the single category A, B, C rule above: a welcome package and free spins for new players are category A.
- **Game catalogue and providers** [iGTL Rule]: dynamic facts from the lobby accessible to an Australian user on the check date. Give only a confirmed number of games: an exact number if counted or stated by the operator; "more than [X]" only if X is confirmed by a count or by the operator. If the number is not confirmed, leave out the quantitative claim.
- **RTP** [Fact]: the RTP of the version at a specific operator, with the source; if only the developer's value is known: "RTP according to the provider: [X]%; the version offered by [Operator] may differ". Do not compare with the RTP of land-based pokies in the states.
- **Online poker** may not be provided to people in Australia in any format.

## Casino and sportsbook: terms do not carry over [iGTL Rule]

An Australian licensee has betting only. For an offshore brand, the casino and the sportsbook have different terms and different grounds for prohibition. Mark the product in the "Scope" field; do not carry one product's terms over to another.

## Lotteries, keno and trade promotions

- Stand-alone material about lotteries and keno is outside the skill's scope.
- **Betting on the outcome of a lottery** is prohibited [Requirement: ACMA].
- **From 01.01.2027** [Requirement: Gambling Reform Act 2026, Schedule 4]: online keno (keno-type lotteries outside a venue) and foreign matched lotteries are prohibited. The exception for trade promotions, per a paraphrase of Schedule 4, has been narrowed (subscription or purchase-based models where the draw is the core of the business may fall outside it); this is a manual check item (⚠ status, `sources.md`). Do not present sweepstakes casinos and "social casinos with cash prizes" as a legal alternative; with such a brief, flag the question in the report.
