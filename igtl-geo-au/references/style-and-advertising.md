# Australian English, formats, advertising, footer, help contacts, final check and reviews

Read this file for any English text for Australia (language, tone, formats), for advertising, promotional and landing-page texts, when taglines, a footer and help contacts are needed, before delivering a finished text (section 7: consistency, generalisations, accuracy of figures, links, claims about work done) and when the text refers to user reviews (section 8).

Sources and dates are in `sources.md`.

## 1. Language

- **Variety:** Australian English, en-AU; dictionary: Macquarie Dictionary [iGTL Default; based on the Australian Government Style Manual]. Spelling: colour, favourite, centre, cheque, travelling, organise, recognise. **licence** is the noun, **license** the verb: "an Australian wagering licence", "licensed bookmaker". **program** in all senses (not programme). The -ise suffix by default; otherwise only per the client's style guide, one variant for the whole text.
- **Betting terminology** [iGTL Default]:

| Not this (GB, US, other markets) | This (AU) |
| :---- | :---- |
| accumulator, acca, parlay | multi |
| bet builder | same game multi (SGM) |
| free bet | bonus bet, or the operator's term |
| betting shop, bookies' shop | TAB, agency, venue, depending on context |
| moneyline (+150), fractional as the main format | decimal odds |
| sportsbook (acceptable, but sounds American) | bookmaker, betting site, online bookie |
| slots (for land-based machines) | pokies |

- **Local realities:** punter (a person who bets), fixed odds, tote, the gallops / the trots / the dogs, AFL, NRL, A-League, State of Origin, Melbourne Cup. "Footy" means AFL or rugby league depending on the state: if the meaning is not clear from context, name the league. For association football, Australians usually say "soccer", while the league uses "football"; follow the client's style guide, consistently.
- **Do not use** British and American regulatory realities: GAMSTOP, UKGC, "£", CAP Code, "sweepstakes casino" as a market model.
- **Address:** "you". Style: plain English, active voice, short sentences.
- **Naturalness:** when translating from another language, keep the meaning but rebuild the sentence. Check for calques, articles, prepositions and word order.
- **Typography:** single quotation marks ‘…’, double for a quote within a quote (Australian Government Style Manual), unless the client's style guide requires otherwise; one system for the whole text. Dashes and the Oxford comma per the style guide, consistently.

## 2. Tone

- [iGTL Default] Write calmly, concretely and informatively. Show value through verified facts: markets, bet types, features, terms.
- Do not put whole phrases in capitals and do not use strings of exclamation marks.
- Avoid empty promotional intensifiers: "MASSIVE", "insane", "unbeatable", "mind-blowing".
- **Urgency and pressure** are two separate questions:
  * **Accuracy of a deadline** [Requirement: ACL]. Give a promotion's deadline only if confirmed, with the time zone: "Offer ends 11:59 pm AEDT, 30 November 2026". An unconfirmed deadline and "Last chance" without a real limit are not allowed.
  * **Calls to bet immediately** [iGTL Rule]. "Bet now!", "Don't miss out", "Get on before the jump!", "Cash out now" do not go into iGTL texts, even with a confirmed deadline. In a remark and the report, call this an iGTL editorial rule. Refer to regulation only if there are signs of it: to the ACL if the urgency is untrue; to s 2.8 of the AANA code if the context shows excessive participation in the sense of the Practice Note (betting beyond one's means, betting as a priority in life, long and frequent betting sessions to build skill). A call-to-action phrase alone, without such context, is not treated as a breach of the code. This restriction concerns only references to s 2.8 when assessing calls to action: the other points of the AANA code (minors, alcohol, promising a win, financial hardship, peer pressure) remain independent grounds for remarks (section 4).
- **Comparative and superlative claims** [Requirement: ACL ss 18, 29]: "best odds", "fastest payouts", "No.1 bookie" only with objective confirmation. No data: describe instead.
- For a CTA use a clear action: "Read the full terms", "Compare the markets", "Visit [Brand]".
- After checking, cut repetition. One restriction does not need a detailed explanation in the introduction, main section, pros and cons, conclusion and FAQ.
- Do not fill a section with generic phrases for the sake of structure.

**Replacement examples:**

* "MASSIVE SIGN-UP BONUS!!!" → the block is deleted (category A, `products.md`, "Inducements"); if needed, neutral category C information.
* "HUGE range of markets!" → "Markets on AFL, NRL, racing and [other confirmed sports]"; the number of sports only if confirmed.
* "Get on now before it's too late!" → "Read the full terms".
* "Risk-free first bet" → deleted entirely (category A and ACL).

Insert numbers and characteristics only from verified sources.

## 3. Formats

| What | Correct | Incorrect |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Currency | "$10", "$2.50", "50c"; if ambiguous, "A$10"; in tables "AUD 10" is acceptable | "10$", "AU$ 10,00" |
| Thousands and decimals | "$10,000", "2.5" | "$10.000", "2,5" |
| Percentages | "96.5%" without a space | "96,5 %" |
| Dates | "13 November 2026"; numeric: day/month/year with slashes: "13/11/2026", "1/7/2026"; ISO only in technical fields | "11/13/2026", "November 13, 2026" |
| Time | one system per text per the client's style guide; for promotions, races and matches with the time zone: "7:30 pm AEDT" | time without a zone in a text for all of Australia |
| Odds | decimal "2.50" or, in the Australian manner, "$2.50"; one variant per text | American "+150"; fractional as the main format |
| Phone | "1800 858 858", "13 11 14" | "+61 1800…" for free-call numbers |
| Age | "18+" | |

- **Time zones** [iGTL Default]: AEST/AEDT (NSW, Victoria, ACT, Tasmania; Queensland on AEST all year), ACST/ACDT (South Australia on ACDT in summer, Northern Territory on ACST all year), AWST (Western Australia). Daylight saving does not apply in every state. For a national text, state the zone explicitly.
- **Amounts** with currency. **Limits** with what is limited and the period: "deposit limit of $[X] per week". Do not mix up a deposit limit with an operator's betting limit.
- Check that the CMS does not insert "10,00 $" and "1.000,00".

## 4. Advertising

**AANA code on wagering advertising** [Requirement: AANA Wagering Advertising Code, February 2021 version; self-regulation, complaints to Ad Standards]. Applies to advertising for licensed operators' products. Advertising must not:
- be directed primarily at minors (under 18); generally does not depict minors;
- depict people aged 18–24 engaged in betting (s 2.3). Placement on a bookmaker's website does not lift this ban. Their presence in the material is acceptable in itself if it does not imply betting: per the Practice Note, for example, an athlete of this age may be shown as the subject of the bet on offer;
- depict, endorse or encourage betting in combination with alcohol consumption;
- encourage excessive participation (s 2.8; per the Practice Note, regular betting, for example every weekend in the season, is not in itself excessive participation);
- present betting as a way to solve financial or personal difficulties;
- directly or indirectly promise a win;
- link betting with sexual success or attractiveness (s 2.7). Per the Practice Note to s 2.7, depicting a person's appearance and self-perception improving after betting may breach the code; the basis depends on context. The broader ban on promising increased self-esteem from betting is an [iGTL Rule];
- depict peer pressure or disparage abstaining from betting.
Do not invent player reviews or stories.

**Commercial nature** [Requirement: AANA Code of Ethics, s 2.7]: advertising must be clearly distinguishable from other content. Material paid for by an operator or built around affiliate links is advertising. Per AANA explanations, a separate label is not automatically required: the commercial nature must be clear to the audience, and if it is not clear from context, a label is needed. How to label is the client's and the platform's decision; if the brief asks for a paid ranking to be presented as an independent editorial assessment, tell the person who set the task.

**ACL** [Requirement: Australian Consumer Law ss 18, 29]: no misleading claims, including through a headline that contradicts the terms ("free", "risk-free"; `products.md`, "Describing promotions in the permitted case").

**From 01.01.2027: IGA Part 7C** [Requirement: Gambling Reform Act 2026, Schedule 1]:
- **What counts as wagering advertising content (s 62C, 62D):** advertising, sponsorship or promotional content relating to a licensed wagering service; including content whose purpose or likely effect is to promote the service, created by the operator, for it or in its interests. An affiliate review with links to the operator most likely falls here. Not such content, in particular: a mention of a licensee's name that does not in itself promote anything (s 62D(3)); content whose sole or main purpose is to discourage betting (s 62D(7)); goods and services of unrelated companies with the same name (s 62D(6)).
- **Content (s 62Z):** the offence is committed by whoever broadcasts such content, or the provider of an online service on which it is provided in Australia, and by whoever authorises or causes it to be shown, if the content is directed at children, depicts children betting, presents betting as a family activity, contains exaggerations about betting, promotes betting as a path to success or achievement, links it with alcohol, is misleading or lacks a responsible gambling message required by Commonwealth, state or territory law.
- **Notable persons (s 62F, 62W):** a licensed operator cannot enter into arrangements for notable persons (current and former athletes, celebrities, influencers, public figures) to take part in promoting wagering advertising and cannot authorise or cause the showing of wagering advertising in which a notable person is presented, seen or heard in connection with promoting it. The exception is advertising that relates exclusively or mainly to racing and is shown on a racing-dedicated program, channel or online service (both conditions). Per ACMA's initial guidance (October 2026), the obligation is on the operator and the rule does not bind the notable person; a person's status as "notable" may arise over time. In the text [iGTL Rule]: do not build betting advertising on quotes, endorsements and the image of well-known people; for affiliate material created in the operator's interests, flag the question in manual checks.
- **Promotion of odds:** the statutory ban on promoting odds concerns broadcasting [per lawyers' commentary; secondary source]. Do not extend it to articles on websites without the primary source.
- **Online services (s 62H, 62P, 62E):** a provider of an online service (websites, apps, social media, streaming) cannot show wagering advertising in Australia unless it has taken reasonable steps to keep it from restricted users: under 18, not age-verified, not logged in to a registered account, or who have opted out of such advertising (triple lock). The ban does not apply to services dedicated to betting or racing (content wholly or principally about them). Per ACMA's initial guidance (November 2026), this exception operates at the level of the whole service, covers the licensee's website and app, but does not extend to the social media pages and profiles of betting and racing companies; a platform relying on triple lock must have an opt-out system for such advertising from 01.01.2027. Whether a particular platform counts as dedicated to betting and how the conditions are met is for the client and their lawyer to decide: flag it in manual checks for a text that will be online after 01.01.2027.
- **Live sport (s 62R, 62RA):** on online services, wagering advertising is prohibited during the broadcast of a sporting event: between 5:00 am and 8:30 pm from 15 minutes before the scheduled start until 5 minutes after the end, including breaks; between 8:30 pm and 5:00 am immediately before the start and after the end. This concerns broadcasts and streams, not ordinary articles; take it into account for promotional texts tied to live content.
- The Wagering Advertising Opt-out Register (IGA Part 7E) will start accepting applications from a day set by Proclamation (s 62ZZC(1)); the law has no fallback date. Give the launch date only after checking the current status of the Proclamation in the task (`SKILL.md`, section 3, "Dynamic norms"); the result of the latest check is in `sources.md`.

**Advertising prohibited services**: `licensing.md`, section 6 (IGA Part 7A). **Inducements** (the single category A, B, C rule): `products.md`.

- Do not address self-excluded people, do not offer benefits for leaving BetStop, do not describe BetStop incorrectly (`player-protection.md`).
- Do not present the outcome of a game of chance as depending on skill ("tricks to beat the pokies").

## 5. Footer of a full piece

In every full review, promotional piece and landing page about betting:

- "18+";
- **tagline**: one of those permitted for websites and digital advertising [Requirement for advertising by licensed operators: NCPF, consistent gambling messaging, since 30.03.2023; for others, iGTL Default]:
  * Chances are you’re about to lose.
  * Think. Is this a bet you really want to place?
  * What’s gambling really costing you?
  * What are you prepared to lose today? Set a deposit limit.
  * Imagine what you could be buying instead.
  * What are you really gambling with?
  The seventh tagline "You win some. You lose more." is permitted only in spoken form (video, radio, phone); do not use it in written text. Copy taglines word for word, with punctuation;
- **call to action:** "For free and confidential support call 1800 858 858 or visit gamblinghelponline.org.au";
- a link to the full terms next to every promotion, if the promotion is described in a permitted way (`products.md`, "Inducements");
- for a confirmed licensee, a licence line (`licensing.md`, section 5);
- a mention of BetStop with the address betstop.gov.au [iGTL Default; licensees must promote BetStop under IGA s 61JP].

Consistent gambling messaging replaced earlier taglines such as "Gamble responsibly" for licensed operators: do not use "Gamble responsibly" as the main message. If the client's style guide sets its own set, compare it with the list above and report any discrepancy.

Do not add a footer to a separately ordered paragraph, FAQ answer or meta tag; if needed, say that the full piece requires one.

For a text about a prohibited service: "18+", the call to action with 1800 858 858; no Australian licence line; do not present the BetStop link as protection from this operator.

## 6. Help contacts

At least one number and link in the footer and in the safer gambling section:

- **Gambling Help Online / Gambling Helpline**: 1800 858 858, free, 24/7, from anywhere in Australia; online chat and email at gamblinghelponline.org.au.
- **BetStop – the National Self-Exclusion Register**: betstop.gov.au.
- **State services** work through the same number 1800 858 858: in NSW, GambleAware (an NSW Government service; not to be confused with the British GambleAware, which has closed), in Victoria, Gambler’s Help. Name a state service only if the text is addressed to that state.
- **Lifeline**: 13 11 14, 24/7 crisis support; appropriate in sections about gambling harm.
- **Checking whether a service is legal**: the ACMA register "Check if a gambling operator is legal".

## 7. Final check of the finished text [iGTL Rule]

Details for the final check stage in `SKILL.md`, section 7.

### Consistency of the article

Find every place where the same fact is repeated: main text, tables, short summaries (overview, at a glance), FAQ, pros and cons, conclusion, meta tags. For each repetition, check:

- **values, units and periods:** $10 and $20, hours and business days, "per day" and "per month", time zones;
- **conditions and exceptions:** a caveat from the main text (including a state exclusion) is not lost in a table or FAQ;
- **verification status:** a fact marked "according to [Operator]" or from a third-party source does not turn into an unqualified statement in a summary or verdict;
- **sequence of events:** the stages of sign-up, verification, deposit, betting, withdrawal and promotion follow the same order in every section (`player-protection.md`, "Player journey"; `products.md`, "Full promotion journey").

Resolve a contradiction using the source conflict procedure (`SKILL.md`, section 3) and fix every place where it occurs. If confirmation is insufficient, mark a material question with `[VERIFY-n]` and leave out an optional statement.

### Generalisations

Find categorical statements: "all", "every", "always", "only", "never", "any", "none", "instant", "guaranteed", "Australia-wide", and their equivalents in a text in another language. For each, check:

- whether the source confirms exactly that scale;
- whether the product category and state the statement relates to are established;
- whether material exceptions are taken into account.

If not confirmed at that scale, narrow the wording to what is verified ("in NSW", "according to [Operator]'s terms") or list the cases for which the statement is confirmed. A single state's rule is not presented as national.

### Accuracy of figures

- Do not present an approximate value as exact: do not turn "around 4,000 markets" into "4,012 markets" or vice versa without a count.
- A lower bound is also a fact: "more than [X]", "over [X]", "[X]+" are allowed only if X is confirmed by a count or a source. If the number is unknown, leave out the quantitative claim; do not replace an unknown number with an approximate one.
- Keep the source's qualifiers: "up to", "usually", "from", "typically", business or calendar days.
- Show rounding as rounding; a range is not reduced to one of its bounds.
- Do not derive figures (sums of stages, percentages, "on average") unless the source states them; the exception is a transparent calculation, for example a turnover example where the input values are visible.

### Links

Every link in the published text:
- opens without an error;
- leads to the stated page: the full terms of the promotion the text is about, the ACMA register, a help page;
- confirms the statement it sits next to;
- belongs to the current Australian version, without redirecting to another GEO and without an archived copy;
- does not lead to a domain blocked by ACMA or its mirror.
Mark a link that could not be checked as a manual check in the report.

### Claims about work done

Wording such as "We tested…", "We signed up…", "We analysed the latest reviews…", "Our research confirms…", "We checked all available payment methods…" is allowed only if the corresponding work was done and its result is recorded in the report: what exactly was done, when, on what sample. Otherwise write about what was actually done: "According to [Operator]'s terms…", "In the reviews we read…". The scale of the claim does not exceed the scale of the work: checking three methods does not allow writing "all payment methods".

## 8. User reviews [iGTL Rule]

Apply when the text summarises player reviews from Trustpilot, ProductReview.com.au, the App Store, Google Play, forums or other platforms.

**Internal record of the sample** (in the fact table):

- platform;
- the date the reviews were read;
- the publication period of the reviews read: the dates of the earliest and the latest;
- number of reviews read;
- the authors' GEO, if known; if not, record that;
- selection method: most recent, by filter (for example, "1 star"), by the platform's sorting ("most relevant").

**Quantitative conclusions.** "Most punters", "the most common complaints", "many reviewers", percentages and shares require a count in the stated sample. With a count: "Of the 50 most recent Trustpilot reviews we read (published 1 September–20 October 2026), 18 mention delayed withdrawals". Without a count: "Among the reviews we read, some punters report delayed withdrawals".

**What users report and facts.** Separate what users write from independently confirmed circumstances. Do not turn complaints into facts about the operator and do not refute them without a source.

**"Recent", "latest"** only after checking the dates: a platform may show old reviews among the most helpful or relevant.

**GEO.** Reviews from other countries may relate to another product version or another licence. Do not attribute them to the Australian version without signs that they concern Australia.

**Platform rating** is a fact about the platform on the check date: give the platform and the date.

**Quotes** word for word, with the platform and publication date, without changing the meaning. Do not invent reviews or compose one from several.
