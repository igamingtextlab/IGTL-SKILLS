# Player protection, verification, payments, complaints and tax

Read this file when the text deals with sign-up and identity verification, the player journey from sign-up to withdrawal, deposits and withdrawals, deposit limits, activity statements, account closure, self-exclusion and BetStop, the credit ban, payment methods, complaints or tax.

The mechanisms below are mandatory for operators with an Australian betting licence. Many National Consumer Protection Framework (NCPF) measures operate through state and territory rules, so details may differ between licensees in different jurisdictions. Each mechanism has its own scope: do not carry the rule of one over to another. Offshore operators are not subject to these measures; do not attribute them to such operators (`licensing.md`, section 6). Sources and commencement dates are in `sources.md`.

## Age and identity

- **Verification before the account is opened** [Requirement: AML/CTF Rules; per AUSTRAC guidance]: since 29.09.2024 all online gambling operators must complete the applicable customer identification procedure (ACIP) before opening an online account or starting to provide any service. From 29.09.2023 to 28.09.2024 a transitional regime allowed deferral in special cases; the former 72-hour window has been removed. Do not describe the current process as "verify within 72 hours" or "verify before your first bet" after the account is opened.
- **Age:** gambling in Australia is 18+. Identity verification includes age verification.
- The specific verification method (documents, electronic verification) is the operator's process [Fact].
- Do not write about a licensee "bet now, verify later", "no ID needed", "no KYC", "instant account without verification".
- About any operator [iGTL Rule]: do not present the absence of identity verification as an advantage. For an offshore operator, describe its actual process as a fact, without calling it "convenient".

## Player journey [iGTL Rule]

If the text describes sign-up, checks, deposit, betting and withdrawal, put these stages into one sequence and check every mention in the main text, tables, FAQ and step-by-step instructions against it.

- The order set by the requirements for a licensee: sign-up application (entering details) → identity and age verification (ACIP) → account opened → offer to set a deposit limit (set one or decline) → deposit → bet → withdrawal. Until verification is complete, no account is opened; "sign up and start betting, verify later" is wrong.
- The same stage is described the same way in every part of the article.
- **Additional checks at withdrawal** are described separately from the initial identity verification: confirming the payment method, a repeat request for documents, AML checks. For each, give the basis in the T&Cs [Fact] and when it arises. Do not create the impression that identity is first checked at withdrawal.
- Check step-by-step instructions ("How to sign up", "How to withdraw") against the same order.

## Deposit limits (voluntary opt-out pre-commitment)

- [Requirement: NCPF, through state rules; in force since May 2019] A licensee offers the customer the option to set a deposit limit at account opening: the customer either sets a limit or expressly declines. The offer is repeated to existing customers (in some jurisdictions at least once a year).
- The limit is binding on the operator: deposits above the set limit are not accepted.
- **Raising a limit** does not take effect immediately. Under the ministers' decision when the NCPF was agreed, after 7 days (cooling-off period) [Requirement: NCPF; source: announcement of the ministers' decision]. The specific period at an operator is a [Fact].
- **Lowering a limit** takes effect immediately at operators [Fact; per operators' terms, the general rule has not been checked by the skill]. Write according to the specific operator's terms.
- Limit periods (for example, a day, a week, 14 days, a month) follow the operator's terms [Fact]; in the ACT, for example, the customer must be able to choose 1, 7, 14 or 28 days.
- The limit is set and changed through the same channels used for betting [per Tasmanian regulator guidance on the NCPF].
- Do not confuse a deposit limit with betting limits the operator sets itself or with account restrictions.

## Activity statements and account history

- [Requirement: NCPF] Monthly activity statements free of charge for active customers, with the net result for the period; the statement for the previous month is available on request or in the account.
- In Victoria, access to transaction history for a period of the customer's choice, at least 7 years [Requirement: Victorian directions]. Do not generalise this period to all of Australia.

## Account closure

- [Requirement: NCPF] Closing an account must be simple; the operator cannot offer a reward for keeping the account open.
- Account closure, an operator time-out and self-exclusion are different tools. Describe the operator's tools under its terms [Fact].

## Self-exclusion and BetStop

- **BetStop – the National Self-Exclusion Register** [Requirement: IGA Part 7B; launched 21.08.2023]:
  * covers all online and phone betting operators with an Australian licence; individual operators cannot be selected;
  * period from 3 months to lifetime; it can be extended at any time but not shortened;
  * early exit not earlier than 3 months after registration, on an application accompanied by a statutory declaration that the person has received counselling or advice about the decision to end self-exclusion from a qualified counsellor, psychologist or medical practitioner; support persons, if nominated, are notified; if the application is not withdrawn, removal takes place 7 days after it is received;
  * **when the chosen period ends, the person is removed from the register automatically**; BetStop gives advance notice of the end. This differs from the British GAMSTOP; do not carry its model over;
  * people can register only themselves; registration is free;
  * a licensee cannot open an account for a registered person, accept a bet from them or send them marketing; existing accounts are closed;
  * BetStop does not replace self-exclusion with an individual operator or state programmes for pubs, clubs and casinos.
- **From 01.01.2027** [Requirement: Gambling Reform Act 2026, Schedule 3]: removal from the register is confirmed no earlier than 7 days after the application (s 61JKA(2)); a licensee closes a registered person's accounts within 7 days (s 61MB); a qualified counsellor is a member of the Psychotherapy and Counselling Federation of Australia or the Australian Counselling Association, or a person specified in the Register rules.
- **Inducements after leaving BetStop** [Requirement: Gambling Reform Act 2026, Schedule 4A; Schedule 5, items 12A, 12D]: a person who left BetStop in the last 90 days is a designated customer, and offering them inducements through direct marketing and on social media is prohibited. These provisions commence on 01.01.2027 but apply from a day the Minister sets; the result of the latest check is in `sources.md`, and the current status of the date is checked in the task (`SKILL.md`, section 3, "Dynamic norms"). Do not write that the ban applies from 01.01.2027.
- **Offshore sites** are not connected to BetStop. Do not write that BetStop protects against them and do not use this as an argument in favour of an offshore operator (`licensing.md`, section 6).
- **Self-exclusion with an operator** exists alongside BetStop; its parameters follow the operator's terms [Fact].

## Credit ban

- [Requirement: IGA Part 2B, s 15C; since 11.06.2024] Licensed online bookmakers do not accept payment by credit card, through an account, wallet or service linked to a credit card, or in cryptocurrency; no credit is provided to customers.
- Write "debit cards". Visa and Mastercard logos do not show the card type.
- The credit card ban under the Interactive Gambling Amendment (Credit and Other Measures) Act 2023 does not extend to lotteries and keno (outside the skill's scope); since June 2026 the ban has been under mandatory review. Do not extend the status of lotteries to betting or vice versa.
- Referring a customer to a lender or payday lender to fund betting is prohibited [Requirement: NCPF].

## Withdrawals

- **Withdrawal times** [Fact]: distinguish the time the operator takes to process the request from the time it takes for the money to arrive. Keep the source's qualifiers: "usually", "up to", business or calendar days. Give the check date: "According to [Operator], withdrawals are usually processed within 24 hours (last checked: …)".
- **Start and end of a period** [iGTL Rule]: for each time frame, record in the fact table which event it runs from and which event ends it. "24 hours after approval" is not "24 hours from request to receipt". Do not add up stage durations into a total unless the operator states one. Do not carry the time frame for one method over to other methods.
- Bank times depend on the method and the bank (for example, instant transfers via PayID/Osko and ordinary transfers): only from the operator's data [Fact].

## Payment methods

- The set of methods, limits, times and fees is a [Fact] from the cashier and payment terms. A payment method neither proves nor disproves the operator's status.
- **Minimums and limits by method** [iGTL Rule]: give the minimum deposit, minimum withdrawal and limits for each method from the cashier or payments page. A minimum deposit from a promotion's terms does not confirm the minimum for each method. No data by method: a `[VERIFY-n]` marker or general wording without a figure.
- **Availability of a method in Australia** [iGTL Rule]: a method is confirmed by the Australian cashier or payments page on the check date. Logos, the .com version, pages for other countries and third-party reviews do not confirm availability. The cashier may depend on device, app or account, so a method missing from the cashier you viewed does not prove it does not exist.
- **Debit cards, PayID/Osko, BPAY, bank transfer, Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal** only if they are in the cashier [Fact]; for wallets at a licensee, take into account the ban on linking to a credit card.
- **Cryptocurrency** is prohibited at licensees since 11.06.2024. At an offshore operator, a [Fact] from the cashier, without presenting it as a way to get around bank restrictions or verification.
- **Offshore operators**: `licensing.md`, section 6. The obligations of banks and payment systems to block payments to illegal operators from 01.01.2027 (Gambling Reform Act 2026, Schedule 2) have ⚠ status in the register: until rechecked, use them only as a risk item in the report, without statements in the text (`sources.md`, "⚠ norms: limits of use"). The AUSTRAC warning about "PayID pokies" is also only context in the report.

## Protection of player funds

- The skill has not established a general mandatory disclosure wording for fund protection like the British scale. How an operator holds customer funds only from its terms or published licence conditions [Fact].
- Do not call funds "fully protected" or "guaranteed" without direct confirmation.

## Complaints

- **Operator first** [per regulator guidance]: a complaint is made under the operator's procedure. For operators licensed in the Northern Territory, the NT Government requires every reasonable effort to resolve the matter with the operator first (and to obtain a dispute number); otherwise the complaint may be rejected. Then the Northern Territory Wagering Commission can be approached (breaches of the law, codes and rules; bet settlement).
- **Licensing authority**: for complaints about a licensee; which authority follows the entry in the ACMA register.
- **ACMA**: for complaints about services not on the register (illegal), about credit being provided and about online in-play at a licensee, and about BetStop breaches.
- **Offshore operators:** per ACMA's warning, Australian law will not help recover money if an illegal service withholds it. Per Victorian Responsible Gambling Foundation guidance, what remains is independent legal advice, a complaint to the ACCC or to the foreign licensing authority.
- [iGTL Rule] Do not call a regulator an ombudsman; the skill has not identified an industry gambling ombudsman in Australia. Describe the operator's specific procedure, response times and escalation under its terms [Fact]. Do not write that ACMA handles individual payout disputes or returns money.

## Tax

- [iGTL Default] Do not describe tax consequences for the player unless the brief directly requires it; if it does, only general wording from an official source without personal conclusions. Per the ATO's position, the winnings of an ordinary player who does not gamble as a business are generally not subject to income tax.
- State point of consumption taxes and fees for using racing and sports information are paid by the operator. Do not use them to explain odds, margin or the size of promotions without confirmation of such a link.
