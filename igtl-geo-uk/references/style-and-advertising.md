# British English, formats, advertising, footer, help contacts, final check and reviews

Read this file for any English text for GB (language, tone, formats), for advertising, promotional and landing texts, when you need the footer, safer gambling message and help contacts, before delivering the finished text (section 7: consistency, generalisations, accuracy of figures, links, claims about work done) and when the text refers to user reviews (section 8).

Sources and dates are in `sources.md`.

## 1. Language

- **Variety:** British English, en-GB. Spelling: colour, favourite, centre, programme, cheque, travelling. **licence** is the noun, **license** the verb: "a Gambling Commission licence", "licensed operator". The -ise or -ize suffix follows the client's style guide, one choice for the whole text.
- **Americanisms and other markets:** soccer → football, parlay → accumulator (acca), moneyline odds (+150) → fractional or decimal. "Sportsbook" sounds like an industry Americanism in a British text; "betting site" or "bookmaker" reads more naturally [iGTL Standard]. Do not use pokies (AU), sweepstakes casino (US) or other models that do not exist in GB.
- **Form of address:** "you". Style: plain English, active voice, short sentences.
- **Natural phrasing:** when translating from another language, keep the meaning but rebuild the sentence. Check for calques, articles, prepositions and word order.
- **Typography:** quotation marks follow the client's style guide (British practice often uses single ‘…’ with double inside), one system for the whole text. Dashes: an en dash with spaces " – ". Oxford comma: per the style guide, consistently.

## 2. Tone

- [iGTL Standard] Write calmly, concretely and informatively. Show the value of the offer through verified facts: bonus size, terms, the games or features available.
- Do not put whole phrases in capitals and do not use strings of exclamation marks.
- Avoid empty promotional intensifiers: "MASSIVE", "insane", "unbeatable", "mind-blowing".
- **Urgency and pressure** [Requirement: CAP guidance on responsibility and problem gambling, 2018]. These are two separate questions:
  * **Accuracy of the deadline.** Give a promotion deadline only if it is confirmed, and state it as a fact: "Offer ends 31 October 2026". An unconfirmed deadline, or "Last chance" with no real limit, is not acceptable.
  * **Pressure to play immediately.** CAP explicitly treats inappropriate urgency as unacceptable, including "Bet Now!" during live events. A confirmed promotion deadline does not justify calls that put undue pressure on people to play immediately: "Bet now!", "Don't miss out", "Hurry, spin now".
- **Comparative and superlative claims** [Requirement: CAP Code, section 3]: "best", "fastest withdrawals", "highest RTP", "No.1" only with objective substantiation that can be produced. Without data, describe instead.
- For CTAs, use a clear action: "Read the full terms", "View the offer", "Visit [Brand]".
- After checking, cut repetition. One restriction does not need a detailed explanation in the introduction, the main section, the pros and cons, the conclusion and the FAQ. Keep a repetition when a section must stand on its own or needs it to make sense.
- Do not fill a section with generic phrases for the sake of structure.

**Replacement examples:**

* "MASSIVE BONUS!!!" → "Welcome offer: 100% match up to £[X], 10x wagering".
* "HUGE game selection!" → "More than [X] slots".
* "Bet now before it's too late!" → "Read the full terms".
* "Risk-free first bet" → "If your first bet loses, you get a £[X] free bet. Free bet stake not returned."

Insert figures and features only from verified sources.

## 3. Formats

| Item | Correct | Incorrect |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Currency | "£10", "£2.50", "50p"; "GBP 10" acceptable in tables | "10£", "10 £", "£10,00" |
| Thousands and decimals | "£10,000", "2.5" | "£10.000", "2,5" |
| Percentages | "96.5%" with no space | "96,5 %" |
| Dates | "22 October 2026", "22/10/2026"; ISO only in technical fields | "10/22/2026", "October 22, 2026" |
| Time | "9pm" or "21:00", one format per text; "midnight", "midday" instead of 12am/12pm | mixing systems in one text |
| Odds | fractional "5/2", "evens" and/or decimal "3.50"; one main format per text | American "+150", "-200" |
| RTP | "RTP of 96.21%" | "96,21 % RTP" |
| Phone | "0808 8020 133" | |
| Age | "18+" | |

- **Amounts** always with the currency. **Limits** with what is limited and the period or event: "maximum stake of £5 per spin", "deposit limit of £[X] per week". Do not mix up a per-spin stake limit with a deposit limit.
- Check that the CMS does not insert "10,00 £" or "1.000,00".

## 4. Advertising [Requirement: CAP Code, sections 16 and 8; BCAP Code, section 17]

- Do not create material of **strong appeal to under-18s** (CAP 16.3.12): characters, celebrities, sportspeople, streamers and gaming aesthetics popular with minors. The CAP and BCAP guidance on this test was updated on 14 October 2025, including on the role of social media; check against it when choosing people and characters for promotional material.
- Do not direct material at people under 18 (CAP 16.3.13).
- **No one who is, or seems to be, under 25** may be shown gambling or playing a significant role (CAP 16.3.14); the exceptions cover only the operator's own transactional platforms. Do not invent player reviews or stories.
- Do not present gambling as a solution to financial concerns (16.3.4) or as an escape from personal, professional or educational problems (16.3.3). Do not claim that gambling improves personal qualities or self-esteem (16.3.6), and do not link it to seduction, sexual success or attractiveness (16.3.8), or to toughness, resilience or recklessness (16.3.9). Do not portray gambling as risk-free or trivialise it.
- Do not promise winnings, and do not present the outcome of a game of chance as depending on skill (for example, "tricks to beat the slots") [Requirement: CAP Code, section 3, misleading claims].
- **Advertising nature** [Requirement: CAP Code, section 2]: material paid for by the operator or built around affiliate links is a marketing communication and must be obviously identifiable as such. How to label it is a decision for the client and the platform; if the brief asks for a paid ranking to be presented as an independent editorial assessment, tell the task owner.
- Do not address self-excluded players, do not offer bonuses for lifting a self-exclusion, and do not describe GAMSTOP as ending automatically.
- Significant conditions of promotions and the rules for "free", "risk-free" and "money back": `products.md`, "Bonuses and promotions".

## 5. Footer of a full piece

In every full review, promotional piece and landing page [iGTL Standard; for a licensee's advertising, some of these points are set by the CAP Code and the IGRG Code, which LCCP Ordinary Code 5.1.8 recommends that licensees follow]:

- "18+"
- a safer gambling message from the client's brand guidelines; if there is none, the neutral "Gambling can be addictive. Please play responsibly." [iGTL Standard]
- a helpline and a help website; in advertising by IGRG Code signatories, a reference to GamCare or gamcare.org.uk (the code also allows GambleAware; see below on its closure)
- "Full T&Cs apply", with significant conditions next to the offer
- for a confirmed licensee, the licence line (`licensing.md`, section 4).

Do not add the footer to a separately ordered paragraph, FAQ answer or meta tag; if needed, note that it belongs in the full piece.

## 6. Help contacts

At least one number and one link, in the footer and in the safer gambling section:

- **National Gambling Helpline (GamCare)**: 0808 8020 133, free, 24/7, the main number for England and Scotland (it also takes calls from Wales); live chat and WhatsApp at gamcare.org.uk.
- **NHS Wales Gambling Helpline**: 0808 281 9265, free, 24/7, for Wales (operating since 1 April 2026).
- **GAMSTOP**: the national self-exclusion scheme for online licensees in GB: gamstop.co.uk.
- **GambleAware** closed on 31 March 2026. Do not present it as an operating organisation. Use a link to gambleaware.org or a mention of BeGambleAware only if the client's brand guidelines or code require it, and warn the task owner about the closure.
- For material addressed to players in Northern Ireland, check the help contacts separately.

## 7. Final check of the finished text [iGTL Standard]

Details for the final check step in `SKILL.md`, section 7.

### Article consistency

Find every place where the same fact appears: the main text, tables, summary boxes (overview, at a glance), FAQ, pros and cons, conclusion, meta tags. For each repetition, check:

- **values, units and periods:** £10 versus £20, hours versus working days, "per day" versus "per month";
- **terms and exceptions:** a qualification in the main text has not been lost in a table or the FAQ;
- **verification status:** a fact marked "according to [Operator]" or taken from a third-party source does not turn into an unqualified statement in a summary or the verdict;
- **sequence of events:** the stages of registration, checks, deposit, play, withdrawal and the bonus promotion appear in the same order in every section (`player-protection.md`, "Player journey"; `products.md`, "Full promotion journey").

Resolve a conflict under the Source Conflict Resolution procedure (`SKILL.md`, section 3) and fix every place it appears. If confirmation is insufficient, mark the material question with a `[VERIFY-n]` marker and remove the non-essential statement. A wording in a table may be shortened as long as no condition, period or attribution is lost.

### Generalisations

Find categorical statements: "all", "every", "always", "only", "never", "any", "none", "instant", "guaranteed", and their equivalents if the source text is in another language. For each, check:

- whether the source confirms exactly that scale;
- whether the product category the statement relates to has been established;
- whether material exceptions have been taken into account.

If it is not confirmed at that scale, narrow the wording to what was checked ("in the [X] slots we checked", "according to [Operator]'s terms") or list the categories for which the statement is confirmed. Example: if one paragraph names roulette, blackjack, Slingo and instant win, that is no basis for attributing one game cycle requirement to all of them; whether a rule applies is checked by each game's category (`products.md`, "Applicability by category").

### Accuracy of figures

- Do not present an approximate value as exact: do not turn "around 4,000 games" into "4,012 games", or the reverse, without a count.
- Keep the source's qualifiers: "up to", "usually", "from", "typically", working or calendar days.
- Present rounding as rounding; a range is not reduced to one of its ends.
- Do not derive figures (stage totals, percentages, "on average") that the source does not state; the exception is a transparent calculation, such as a wagering example where the inputs are visible.

### Links

Check that links are reliable, not just that they are present. Each link in the published text:
- opens without an error;
- leads to the page it claims to: the full terms of the promotion the text is about, the register entry of the right operator;
- supports the statement it sits next to;
- relates to the current British version, with no redirect to another GEO and no archived copy.
Flag a link that could not be checked as a manual check in the report.

### Claims about work done

Phrases such as "We tested…", "We signed up…", "We analysed the latest reviews…", "Our research confirms…", "We checked all available payment methods…" are allowed only if the work was done and its result is recorded in the report: what exactly was done, when, and on what sample. Otherwise, write about what was actually done: "According to [Operator]'s terms…", "In the reviews we read…". The scale of the claim does not exceed the scale of the work: checking three methods does not entitle you to write "all payment methods".

## 8. User reviews [iGTL Standard]

Apply this when the text summarises player reviews from Trustpilot, the App Store, Google Play, forums or other platforms.

**Internal record of the sample** (in the fact table):

- the platform;
- the date the reviews were read;
- the publication period of the reviews read: the dates of the earliest and the latest;
- the number of reviews read;
- the authors' GEO, if known; if not, record that;
- the selection method: most recent, by filter (for example, "1 star"), by the platform's sorting ("most relevant").

**Quantitative conclusions.** "Most players", "the most common complaints", "many reviewers", percentages and shares require a count in a defined sample. With a count: "Of the 50 most recent Trustpilot reviews we read (published 1 September–20 October 2026), 18 mention delayed withdrawals". Without a count: "Among the reviews we read, some players report delayed withdrawals".

**User reports and facts.** Keep what users write separate from independently confirmed circumstances: "Some reviewers say their withdrawal took more than a week" describes reviews; the processing time in the operator's terms describes a fact. Do not turn complaints into facts about the operator, and do not refute them without a source.

**"Recent", "latest", "newest"**: use them only after checking the dates, because a platform may show old reviews among the most useful or relevant. Call a sample "the latest reviews" only if the reviews were sorted by date and read consecutively for a stated period; this must be clear from the internal record.

**GEO.** Reviews from other countries may relate to a different version of the product or a different licence. Do not attribute them to the British version without signs that they concern GB.

**Platform rating** (for example, the average score on Trustpilot) is a fact about the platform on the date of the check: give the platform and the date.

**Quotes** are given verbatim, with the platform and publication date, without changing the meaning. Do not invent reviews or combine several into one (section 4).
