# Player protection, checks, payments and taxes

Read this file when the text deals with registration, verification, the player journey from registration to withdrawal, deposits and withdrawals, limits, self-exclusion, GAMSTOP, financial checks, payment methods, protection of customer funds or taxes.

Each mechanism has its own scope: do not carry one mechanism's rule over to another. Sources and effective dates are in `sources.md`.

## Age and identity

These are two different requirements [Requirement]:

- **Age** (LCCP SR 3.2.11, since 7 May 2019): a licensee verifies age before the customer can deposit, access free-to-play versions of games on the licensee's site, or gamble with their own money, a free bet or a bonus.
- **Identity** (LCCP 17.1.1): a licensee verifies name, address and date of birth before the customer is permitted to gamble.
- Before deposit, licensees **should** tell the customer which documents or information may be needed, when, and how to provide them. This is recommended practice, not a firm condition.
- When a customer requests a withdrawal, a licensee cannot require for the first time information it could reasonably have requested earlier, unless another legal obligation requires it. Do not describe "document checks only at withdrawal" as a licensee's normal practice.
- A specific operator may verify everything earlier, for example at registration. That is its own process [Fact], not a general requirement.
- Do not write "no verification casino", "play without ID" or "no KYC" about a licensee.

## Player journey [iGTL Standard]

If the text describes registration, checks, deposit, play and withdrawal, put these stages into one sequence and check every mention in the main text, tables, FAQ and step-by-step instructions against it.

- The order set by the requirements: age check before deposit, demo play and gambling (SR 3.2.11); identity check before gambling (LC 17.1.1). The operator may check earlier, for example at registration; that is its own process [Fact].
- The same stage is described the same way in every part of the article. Resolve a conflict such as "you can play straight after signing up; documents are needed only at withdrawal" in one section and "all players are verified before they play" in another according to the requirements and the operator's terms.
- **Additional checks at withdrawal** are described separately from the initial identity check: payment method verification, a further request for documents, source of funds checks, financial checks. For each, give the basis in the T&Cs [Fact] and the point at which it arises. Do not give the impression that identity is checked for the first time at withdrawal (LC 17.1.1, above).
- Check step-by-step instructions ("How to sign up", "How to withdraw") against the same order: the steps must not skip or reorder the checks.

## Financial checks

- **Financial Vulnerability Checks** [Requirement: LCCP SR 3.4.4, in force since 30 August 2024]: a check is mandatory when a customer's deposits minus withdrawals exceed **£150 in a rolling 30-day period** (threshold in force since 28 February 2025; previously £500). The minimum is a check of public records: bankruptcy, county court judgments (CCJs), IVAs, Debt Relief Orders and equivalents. The operator is not required to repeat this check when the threshold is reached if a Financial Vulnerability Check or a Financial Risk Assessment was carried out in the previous 12 months. This is not a ban on repeating the check. The rule applies to remote licences, with exceptions, including remote lottery licences.
  * For the text: do not promise "no checks" or "no affordability checks", do not promise that the check will be a one-off, and do not describe the threshold as the operator's choice. How the operator carries out the check and which documents it may request come from its T&Cs [Fact].
- **Financial Risk Assessments**: on 7 July 2026 the Gambling Commission approved their staged introduction. As of 22 October 2026, the start date of the first stage has not been announced. Do not describe the thresholds of future stages as in force until an official source confirms it.

## Deposit and other financial limits (RTS 12)

- Since 31 October 2025 [Requirement: RTS 12A, 12C, 12E]: the player is prompted to set a limit at registration or at first deposit; setting a limit is the default option, and declining requires an action by the player. Links to the limit settings appear on the homepage and on the deposit pages.
- From **30 September 2026** [Requirement: RTS 12B; the second phase was moved from 30 June 2026, announced on 26 May 2026]:
  * the operator must offer a gross deposit limit: a limit on the total amount deposited in a period;
  * "deposit limit" means only that limit. Name limits on stakes, losses or deposits minus withdrawals the way the operator names them: stake limit, loss limit, net deposit limit;
  * the gross deposit limit is shown at least as prominently as other limit types and offered over fixed periods; the periods must include 24 hours, 7 days and one month;
  * once the limit is reached, further deposits are blocked until the new period starts or until an increase requested by the player takes effect.
- A limit increase happens only at the player's request, after a cooling-off period of at least 24 hours and a further confirmation at the end of that period; a decrease takes effect immediately [Requirement: RTS 12D].
- Do not confuse the deposit limit with the statutory stake limit on slots (£5/£2).

## Time-outs and self-exclusion

- **Time-out** [Requirement: LCCP 3.3.4]: a break of 24 hours, one week, one month, or another period at the player's request of up to 6 weeks.
- **Self-exclusion with the operator: mandatory actions** [Requirement: LCCP SR 3.5.3]: the operator has a self-exclusion procedure; after a self-exclusion it closes the account, returns the funds, prevents the person from gambling and stops marketing; when setting it up, it signposts the person to support services.
- **Self-exclusion with the operator: recommended parameters** [Ordinary Code 3.5.4; the Gambling Commission describes this as good practice that does not have the status of a licence condition, and the operator may reasonably take a different approach]: a minimum period offered of 6 to 12 months; extension on request for periods of at least 6 months; options including periods of up to at least five years; after the period ends, the self-exclusion stays in place for at least seven more years unless the person takes action to gamble again; returning requires a phone call or contact in person and one day to cool off. Describe the specific operator's periods from its terms [Fact] and do not present the recommended parameters as mandatory.
- **GAMSTOP** [Requirement: LCCP SR 3.5.5]: licensees covered by this rule must take part in the national self-exclusion scheme. The rule has exceptions, including remote lottery licences that do not offer instant-win lotteries. So write "online operators licensed in Great Britain must be part of GAMSTOP" only about casino, betting and bingo; check lotteries separately.
  * Periods: 6 months, 1 year, 5 years, or 5 years with automatic renewal. It cannot be cancelled during the chosen period.
  * After the period ends, the self-exclusion **does not end automatically**: to remove it, the player calls GAMSTOP, and then a 24-hour waiting period applies.
  * GAMSTOP does not cover high street bookmakers (they have a separate scheme, MOSES) or unlicensed sites.

## Withdrawals

- A withdrawal request cannot be cancelled [Requirement: RTS 14B, since 31 October 2021]. Do not write "cancel your withdrawal and keep playing".
- **Withdrawal times** [Fact]: distinguish the operator's processing time for the request (pending period) from the time for the money to arrive. Keep the source's qualifiers: "usually", "up to", working or calendar days. Give the check date: "According to [Operator], withdrawal requests are usually processed within 24 hours (last checked: …)".
- **Start and end of the period** [iGTL Standard]: for each time limit, record in the fact table the event it runs from and the event it ends with. Typical stages: request submitted, checked and approved, payment sent, money received. "24 hours after approval" is not the same as "24 hours from request to receipt". Name the period explicitly in the text: "processed within 24 hours of approval", "funds usually arrive within [X] working days after processing". Do not add stage times into a total unless the operator states one. Do not carry one method's time over to other methods.

## Payment methods

- The set of methods, limits, times and fees is a [Fact] from the cashier and the payment terms. A payment method neither proves nor disproves the operator's status.
- **Minimums and limits by method** [iGTL Standard]: give the minimum deposit, minimum withdrawal and limits for each method from the cashier or the payments page. A minimum deposit in a promotion's terms belongs to that promotion and does not confirm the minimum for every method. No data by method: a `[VERIFY-n]` marker or a general wording without a figure.
- **Method availability in GB** [iGTL Standard]: a method is confirmed by the British cashier or the British payments page on the date of the check. Logos, the .com version, pages for other countries and third-party reviews do not confirm availability in GB. The cashier may depend on the device, app or account, so the absence of a method in the cashier you viewed is no proof that the method does not exist. If there is no information about a method, do not infer it by analogy with other methods or operators: leave the method out, or use `[VERIFY-n]` if the method is key to the text.
- **Credit cards** [Requirement: LCCP 6.1.2, since 14 April 2020]: licensees may not accept credit card payments for remote gambling, including through e-wallets and other payment services if the wallet is funded by credit card. Do not write "deposit with your credit card". Name "debit cards": Visa and Mastercard logos do not tell you the type of card. Unlike in Germany, the ban on credit cards here is direct.
- **E-wallets** (PayPal, Skrill, Neteller and others) [Fact]: availability and bonus exclusions for individual methods come from the cashier and the bonus terms.
- **Pay by mobile, Apple Pay, Google Pay, open banking / Pay by Bank, Trustly**: only if they appear in the cashier [Fact].
- **Paysafecard:** distinguish the prepaid voucher with a PIN from the personal account. A voucher can fund the account, but you cannot withdraw to it. At a licensee, age and identity checks are mandatory in any case: do not describe the method as anonymous or as a way around verification. The payment procedure, withdrawal availability, limits and fees come from the operator's and the payment service's materials [Fact].
- **Cryptocurrencies**: only if they appear in the cashier [Fact]. For a licensee, the presence of the method does not replace the verification and source of funds requirements; if it is unclear how the method fits with them, tell the task owner and draw no conclusions about whether it is permitted.

## Protection of customer funds

- [Requirement: LCCP 4.2.1] The operator states in its T&Cs whether customer funds are protected in the event of insolvency, using the Gambling Commission rating: not protected, medium or high protection. The player acknowledges this before gambling with deposited funds.
- The specific operator's level is a [Fact]: write it as the terms state it. Do not call the funds "fully protected" or "guaranteed": even medium protection, as the Gambling Commission describes it, gives no absolute guarantee.

## Complaints and ADR

- [Requirement: LCCP SR 6.1.1] A licensee has a procedure for handling customer complaints and disputes and publishes clear and accessible information on how to complain. If a dispute is not resolved through that procedure within 8 weeks of receiving the complaint, the player can refer it to ADR. The ADR provider is accredited by the Secretary of State under the DMCC Act 2024, and its services are free to the player. The operator's terms cannot restrict the player's right to go to court.
- [per Gambling Commission guidance] The player first goes through the operator's complaints procedure; ADR comes after it. According to the Gambling Commission's consultation response on the DMCC Act, it no longer approves ADR providers: accreditation is granted by the Secretary of State, and the Gambling Commission refers to the list of accredited providers.
- The operator's specific ADR provider, response times and escalation route come from its T&Cs or complaints page [Fact]. Do not fill in a provider by analogy with other operators.
- [iGTL Standard] Do not generalise about whether an ADR decision is binding on the parties: describe it according to the specific provider's rules and the operator's T&Cs. Do not call an ADR provider an ombudsman: an industry Gambling Ombudsman had not been set up as of the skill's update date. Do not write that the Gambling Commission handles individual complaints or recovers money without confirmation from its official materials.

## Taxes

- [iGTL Standard] Do not describe tax consequences for the player unless the brief explicitly requires it; if it does, use only a general wording based on an official source (GOV.UK, HMRC), with no personal conclusions.
- Remote Gaming Duty (40% since 1 April 2026) and General Betting Duty are paid by the operator. Do not use them to explain prices, odds, RTP or bonus size without confirmation of such a link.
