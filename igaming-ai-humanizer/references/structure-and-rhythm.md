# Structure, rhythm, punctuation

## Rhythm

- Vary sentence and paragraph length the way the content naturally calls
  for — short where the point is simple, longer where it needs room. Don't
  run several sentences or paragraphs of near-identical length back to
  back; that uniformity, not any specific word count, is the marker.
- Don't default to intro → 3–4 identical paragraphs → list → conclusion.
- Don't restate the intro's content as the closing summary.

## Lists and repeated structure

- Don't split a thought into "First... Second... Third..." unless a list
  was explicitly requested.
- Watch for forced symmetry — the same number of examples, same sentence
  shape, same length, repeated block after block for no reason tied to the
  content. Let the count and shape follow the facts, not a pattern.
- Don't spread numbers and data evenly "for effect" — place them where the
  content actually needs them.

## Punctuation

- Em dash: no more than 1 per 300–400 words.
- Don't default to bold-first bullets ("**Heading:** text") as the standard
  list format unless that's the project's established style.
- Don't use emoji as list markers unless the brand's own style calls for it.
- Don't drop a colon purely for effect, with no real enumeration/explanation
  after it.
- Follow the project's own punctuation style guide where one exists
  (e.g. Oxford comma usage). Where there is no fixed house rule, don't force
  the same punctuation choice into every single list out of habit.

## Content

- Add specifics: numbers, conditions, mechanics — instead of superlatives
  with no backing. Use only facts that are actually known or provided;
  never invent a number or condition to sound concrete.
- Keep genuine caveats and conditions that exist in the source material
  ("good for X, not for Y") instead of smoothing them into uniform
  positivity — but don't fabricate a caveat that isn't true just to seem
  more human.
- Keep real, relevant detail in examples rather than trimming them down to
  a too-perfect fit for the thesis.
- Don't compare with competitors in the abstract — only via a fact, number,
  or condition.
- Don't close with a stock polite phrase or a self-reference to being
  generated.
