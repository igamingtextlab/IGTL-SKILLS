# English AI-text markers

## Words (avoid as generic marketing filler)

These aren't banned outright — they're fine in a precise, non-decorative use
(e.g. "state-of-the-art" in a technical spec sheet with real benchmarks).
Avoid them as default superlative filler with nothing behind them:

delve, delve into, leverage, harness, navigate, utilize, empower, resonate,
revolutionize, embrace, embark, unlock, streamline, elevate, tapestry,
ecosystem, paradigm, synergy, journey, myriad, landscape, ever-evolving
landscape, realm, testament, symphony, plethora, array, capabilities,
valuable insights, pivotal, seamless, robust, transformative,
groundbreaking, vibrant, bustling, keen, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art,
top-tier, top-notch, premium, high-quality, comprehensive, exciting,
thrilling, unique, standout, efficient, tailored, straightforward,
effortlessly, swiftly, enthusiasts, to reveal, to feature, to boast,
to provide, offers, allow, maximize, boost, stands as

## Phrases (avoid as generic filler)

complex and multifaceted; intricate interplay; played a crucial role;
it's important to note; navigating the complexities; in summary;
in conclusion; whether you're X or Y; when it comes to; in the world of;
I recently had the pleasure; I hope this helps; that's a great question

## Structure

- Don't default to intro → 3–4 paragraphs → list → conclusion.
- Don't build a "listicle in a trenchcoat" (The first reason is… The second
  reason is…) unless a list was explicitly requested.
- Don't restate the same summary at paragraph, section, and conclusion
  level.
- Don't run sentences or paragraphs at a uniform length — vary pacing the
  way the content naturally calls for, rather than keeping every unit the
  same size.
- Don't present every claim with the same uniform confidence if the source
  material actually contains a caveat or condition — keep it in, don't
  smooth it away.
- Don't construct examples/anecdotes that fit the thesis too perfectly if
  that means cutting real, relevant detail to make them tidier.

## Punctuation: em dash

- Cap: no more than 1 em dash per 300–400 words.
- Model baselines (em dashes per 1,000 words): human ~2.5–2.75; GPT-4o
  ~1.05; Gemini ~5.97; Claude 4.8 Opus ~11.98.
- The dash alone is not a sufficient signal — cap its frequency, don't use
  it as the sole criterion.

## Other

Switching the model's built-in writing style (Concise/Explanatory/Formal,
custom styles) does not remove AI-detectability on its own — change
structure, rhythm, and add specifics instead of relying on lexicon alone.
